# Machine-learning model attribution

Budget Planner ships four pretrained models. None of them is part of the
initial page load: each is fetched from this site's own origin the first
time the feature that needs it is used (`www/src/ocrWorker.js`,
`www/src/voiceWorker.js`), then run entirely in the browser via
[`rten`](https://github.com/robertknight/rten).

Two of the four were **converted to the `.rten` model format** (the files
ending `.rten`), which changes the serialization format only — no change
to model architecture, and no change to trained weights.
`all-MiniLM-L6-v2.onnx` ships as the ONNX file it was downloaded as, byte
for byte.

The two voice models are the exception, and the one place a weight
actually changes: they ship as ONNX with **their weights cast to
float16**, which halves each download from ~138MB to ~69MB. This is a
lossy cast and is stated as such. The arithmetic is not f16: `rten`
widens f16 constants back to f32 when it loads the model
(`convert_f16_constant`), so inference runs in f32 as before. It was
measured rather than assumed — on a 48-clip corpus per language, every
one of the 96 transcripts came back byte-identical to the f32 model's,
at unchanged parsed-transaction accuracy.

Where a license requires a statement of modification (CC-BY-4.0
§3(a)(1)(B), Apache-2.0 §4(b)), those two conversions — format, and the
f16 cast — are the modifications, and they are the only ones.

The full license *text* for every component, models included, is
generated by `cargo about` into `www/static/third-party-licenses.html`
and linked from the app's privacy page.

## WeNet LibriSpeech U2++ Conformer (English)

**File:** `www/static/voice/en-wenet-f16.onnx`
**Used for:** English voice entry (`crates/budget-calc/src/voice.rs`)
**Source:**
[csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-en-wenet-librispeech](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-en-wenet-librispeech),
an ONNX re-export of
[WeNet's own `librispeech_u2pp_conformer_exp`](https://wenet.org.cn/downloads?models=wenet)

**License: CC BY 4.0**, inherited from the training corpus.
[WeNet's pretrained-model documentation](https://github.com/wenet-e2e/wenet/blob/main/docs/pretrained_models.md)
states the rule verbatim: *"The pretrained model in WeNet follows the
license of it's corresponding dataset."* This model's dataset is
[LibriSpeech](https://www.openslr.org/12/), whose openslr page gives
`License: CC BY 4.0`. The WeNet toolkit that produced it is Apache-2.0.
The HuggingFace re-upload declares no license of its own, which is why
the chain above is traced to the corpus rather than read off the mirror.

Required attribution:

> English speech recognition uses a WeNet U2++ Conformer trained on
> LibriSpeech. LibriSpeech is
> [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) — V. Panayotov,
> G. Chen, D. Povey and S. Khudanpur, "Librispeech: An ASR corpus based on
> public domain audio books", ICASSP 2015.

**Modification:** the CTC-branch ONNX export had its weights cast to
float16 (`onnxconverter_common.float16`, `keep_io_types=True`); the graph
is otherwise the export as downloaded. No change to architecture. The
weights are the one thing that did change, and only in precision.

## Models these replaced

`QuartzNet15x5` (English) and `Citrinet-512` (Mandarin), both from the
NVIDIA NGC catalogue, shipped first and were removed for licensing, not
for quality. Their NGC model cards stated only that *"License to use this
model is covered by the NGC TERMS OF USE unless another License/Terms Of
Use/EULA is clearly specified"*, and neither had a HuggingFace mirror
carrying an explicit license tag. This app does not merely *use* the
weights — it re-hosts them as static files on its own origin so the
browser can fetch them, which is redistribution and needs a license that
permits it. Sibling models in the same NeMo family are tagged CC-BY-4.0,
which is suggestive but is not a statement about these two.

`u2pp-Conformer-Yue` (Cantonese) shipped next and was withdrawn, also
for licensing. Its own model card is Apache-2.0, but the licence chain
breaks one level further down: the weights were trained on
[WenetSpeech-Yue](https://huggingface.co/datasets/ASLP-lab/WenetSpeech-Yue),
whose dataset card is **CC BY-NC 4.0** — NonCommercial. WeNet's own rule
is that *"the pretrained model in WeNet follows the license of it's
corresponding dataset"*, which makes the permissive tag on the model card
the wrong one to rely on. The break is invisible unless the dataset card
is read; the mirror this app fetched from
(`csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-wenetspeech-yue-...`) declares no licence at
all. No smaller or differently-licensed Cantonese model was found to
replace it — `Conformer-Yue` at 130M parameters is simultaneously the
smallest and the most accurate of the candidates surveyed — so Cantonese
voice entry is not offered rather than offered on a worse model.

An int8 quantized export of Citrinet-512 was measured and rejected (it
returned confidently empty transcripts on every input, at full speed,
against an fp32 control that scored 100% on identical features). That
finding is about `rten`'s int8 path rather than about any one model, and
is why neither shipped voice model is quantized. f16 is a different
thing and is safe: it is a storage format that `rten` widens back to f32
before any arithmetic happens, which is why it costs nothing, whereas
int8 changes what the operators themselves do.
